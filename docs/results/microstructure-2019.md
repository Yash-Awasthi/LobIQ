## Order flow imbalance (Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov, 2014)

OFI sums the order flow at the best quotes over 10 s intervals; the model is dmid = beta OFI. Per-symbol beta and in-sample R2 come from the train days; out-of-sample R2 uses that beta on the test days. The universal model replaces each symbol's beta by c / depth^lambda, two numbers fitted on half-hour windows of all train symbol-days (660 windows): lambda = 1.03, R2 of the log fit 0.91. The paper reports lambda close to 1.

## Micro-price (Stoikov, 2018)

States: top-of-book imbalance bid / (bid + ask) in 10 buckets, times spread of 1 or 2 ticks, sampled at every change of the best quotes. The adjustment G(state) = sum of g_k (g_1 = (1 - Q)^-1 R, g_k+1 = (1 - Q)^-1 T g_k, eight terms) is estimated per symbol from the symmetrised train transitions. Each predictor is scored by its mean squared error against the mid at the next mid change and the mid 1 s later; the table gives the error reduction against the mid itself (higher is better, 0 means no better than the mid). Only symbols quoted at 1-2 ticks for at least half of their quote changes are shown (large-tick stocks, where the model applies).

## Queue position (Moallemi and Yuan, 2017)

Every limit order that joins the best bid or ask while the spread is one tick, on the same 20 large-tick symbols over all 3 days, by the shares already queued ahead of it relative to the symbol's typical best-level size (an exponential average of (bid + ask size) / 2). Filled: at least one execution before the order is cancelled, deleted or replaced. Markout: the mid 1 s after the first fill against the fill price, per share, in ticks (+0.5 is the half spread kept with no move; below 0 the fill lost money). Value per order: fill rate times markout.

## Trade costs (SEC Rule 605 style)

Every visible execution in the window on the 30 symbols, signed by its taker's side (the resting order's opposite). Effective half-spread: the price against the mid just before the trade; realized spread: against the mid 5 s later, what the resting side kept; price impact: how far the mid moved with the taker in those 5 s. Effective = realized + impact. Basis points, weighted by shares.

## VPIN and volatility

VPIN (Easley, Lopez de Prado and O'Hara 2012) from exact trade signs: each symbol-day's visible executions filled into 500 equal-volume buckets, VPIN the mean of |buy - sell| / V over the last 50. Andersen and Bondarenko (2014) argue its link to volatility is mechanical: compare its correlation with the absolute mid return of the next bucket against that of the current one. ## Volatility signature

Realized variance of the mid over the window, sampled every k seconds (averaged over the k sampling offsets), divided by the same at 300 s. Without microstructure noise the ratio would be 1 at every k; bid-ask bounce and tick discreteness push it away at short k (Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Labys 2000). Medians over the 30 symbols; window volatility from the 300 s sampling. RV(300 s) rests on 66 returns per symbol-day, so each ratio carries roughly 17% sampling error; a ratio below 1 means moves continued rather than reversed at the longer scale.

## Self-excitation of mid-price changes (Hawkes)

An exponential Hawkes process (intensity mu + alpha sum exp(-beta (t - t_i))) fitted by maximum likelihood to the mid-change times of each symbol in each half-hour window with at least 200 changes (`fit_hawkes`, checked against simulated processes in tests/microstructure_test.cpp). The branching ratio n = alpha / beta is the share of changes triggered by earlier ones; Filimonov and Sornette (2012) report it rising toward 1 over the 2000s. 1 / beta is how long the excitation lasts. Half-hour windows limit the intraday seasonality that can masquerade as excitation.

## Caveats

- ITCH is Nasdaq's book only; the consolidated quote is tighter, so depth and imbalance are partial.
- The most active names are mostly ETFs and leveraged ETFs; two test days measure stability across days, not across regimes.
- OFI explains the contemporaneous price change (as in the paper); it is not a forecast. The micro-price is one: it uses only the book at the time of the prediction.
