## Order flow imbalance (Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov, 2014)

OFI sums the order flow at the best quotes over 10 s intervals; the model is dmid = beta OFI. Per-symbol beta and in-sample R2 come from the train days; out-of-sample R2 uses that beta on the test days. The universal model replaces each symbol's beta by c / depth^lambda, two numbers fitted on half-hour windows of all train symbol-days (660 windows): lambda = 1.01, R2 of the log fit 0.94. The paper reports lambda close to 1.

## Micro-price (Stoikov, 2018)

States: top-of-book imbalance bid / (bid + ask) in 10 buckets, times spread of 1 or 2 ticks, sampled at every change of the best quotes. The adjustment G(state) = sum of g_k (g_1 = (1 - Q)^-1 R, g_k+1 = (1 - Q)^-1 T g_k, eight terms) is estimated per symbol from the symmetrised train transitions. Each predictor is scored by its mean squared error against the mid at the next mid change and the mid 1 s later; the table gives the error reduction against the mid itself (higher is better, 0 means no better than the mid). Only symbols quoted at 1-2 ticks for at least half of their quote changes are shown (large-tick stocks, where the model applies).

## Caveats

- ITCH is Nasdaq's book only; the consolidated quote is tighter, so depth and imbalance are partial.
- The most active names are mostly ETFs and leveraged ETFs; two test days measure stability across days, not across regimes.
- OFI explains the contemporaneous price change (as in the paper); it is not a forecast. The micro-price is one: it uses only the book at the time of the prediction.
